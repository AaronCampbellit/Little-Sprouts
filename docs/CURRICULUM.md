# Little Sprouts learning progression

Researched 15 November 2026. This is a proposed game activity sequence informed by early-years guidance, rather than a standardized curriculum or a developmental assessment.

## Evidence and interpretation

The [Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-HE23_1100-PURL-gpo65456/pdf/GOVPUB-HE23_1100-PURL-gpo65456.pdf), printed pages 47, 55, and 57–60, describes toddler matching and sorting; emerging counting of small collections; and, at 36–48 months, beginning number sequences toward ten and awareness of familiar letters. Coordinating one spoken number with each object is a separate skill from reciting the sequence. These broad progressions support offering all numbers and letters while adjusting assistance and choices.

[Development Matters](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/development-matters--2/development-matters), Department for Education, updated September 2023, emphasizes individual pathways and depth of understanding. Its three/four-year guidance includes small-quantity recognition, counting and numeral relationships through five, shape exploration, language, rhymes, and beginning awareness of sounds. Its birth-to-three guidance spans a wider range than our two-to-three stage, so our stage is an adaptation rather than a direct age checklist.

**Our implementation decision:** expose 1–10 and the complete alphabet immediately in Explore. Offer supported object counting to ten in the default growing stage; allow a parent to select five. Full alphabet mastery and independent counting of ten objects are not expectations imposed on every three-year-old.

## Proposed activity sequence

These are our original game-design adaptations. Ages suggest an entry point; a parent can choose any available setting.

| Area | Little steps, around 2–3 | Growing discoveries, around 3–4 | Available content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Numbers | Count together; touch an object once; select between two answers | Count each object, connect the total with a numeral, choose among three answers | 1–10; practice limit 5 or 10 |
| Letters | Explore with an adult, repeat names, match identical shapes | Find a named letter; explore upper/lowercase and familiar example words | All A–Z, upper/lower/together |
| Colors | Match two choices; compare familiar colors | Name and distinguish a larger palette | Red, blue, yellow, green, orange, purple, pink, brown, black, white |
| Shapes | Match circles, squares, triangles, rectangles | Explore a larger set; talk about sides and corners with an adult | Circle, square, triangle, rectangle, oval, star, heart, diamond |
| Our world | Match pictures; name everyday objects | Listen for a familiar noun and find its picture; discuss use and category together | Apple, banana, ball, teddy bear, cat, dog, bird, fish, car, cup, shoe, tree |

Diamond is an everyday shape name in this activity. A later geometry module can introduce rhombus and rotated squares explicitly. Stars and hearts are familiar shapes, not a complete mathematical taxonomy.

Letters are currently letter-name and visual-recognition activities, not a systematic phonics course. X uses fox as an ending-sound example. We do not imply that xylophone begins with the usual /ks/ sound or use speech synthesis to pronounce isolated phonemes.

## A sample shared session

1. Let the child choose a companion and subject.
2. Explore a few items together, naming and noticing them.
3. Play a five-question round if they are interested. Count the objects together; repeat the prompt or use a hint freely.
4. Let the child choose when to continue and when to finish. There is no timer, failure screen, penalty, or speed reward.
5. Carry one idea into real play: count snacks; find a round plate; spot a letter in a book; sort toy animals.

Progress records completed responses, first tries, retries, and explicit hints. These observations do not establish mastery or diagnose development. Themes share the same learning records.

## Motion requirement

Menus and pickers respond with short fades; simple questions change gently without a long wait. World changes remain gradual, and future action scenes must avoid rapid cuts, flashes, sudden camera motion, or forced speed. Counting marks and feedback stay in place. Reduced motion removes decorative movement. Questions advance only when the child chooses to continue.

## Future teaching activities

Add quantity comparison, sorting by one attribute, size and position words, simple repeating patterns, rhyming, story conversations, and initial-sound listening as separate modules. A later word activity should begin with oral vocabulary and meaningful pictures before independent decoding. Expanded activities should get their own objectives, evidence, and adult-supported examples.
